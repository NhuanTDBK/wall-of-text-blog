The visuals drew me into Briana (Mazzio) May’s HUMAN.md. I found myself agreeing with most of it. I keep coming back to “own the output. fully.” and “find the thing in the weird place.” [HUMAN.md](https://www.human-md.ai/)

Owning the output means I can explain why I kept it. If AI writes a paragraph and I publish it under my name, I chose every sentence that made it through. If a reader points out a mistake, I owe them a correction. “AI wrote it” tells them where the words came from. They still came to me for an answer.

Imagine finding a hand-drawn map on a bus stop sign. A stranger drew a crooked arrow around a flooded corner. You can ask AI to generate a hundred maps. The arrow gives me an idea for a story about a person who cared enough to help whoever came next.

That small act of caring is the kind of thing that makes me want to write. A model can read a pulse from a sensor and describe an adrenaline rush. It has no pulse of its own. When I hover over Publish and my heart speeds up, the model cannot feel what is at stake for me. Sometimes that feeling tells me to cut a line. Sometimes it tells me I finally wrote what I meant. I have to decide which it is.
