## A weight applies to a prediction for one viewer

Phoenix predicts several actions and continuous quantities for each viewer and post. A score can combine a probability of liking, a probability of reporting, and an estimate of time spent. In the additive path, the value model multiplies each prediction by its weight and sums the terms. A post's raw counts may be available elsewhere in the pipeline. The inputs to these weighted terms are predictions for the request. [August 14 explanation](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/269340ce2eb8ca785bafa4f2c17a2e8b752bccd3/README.md#august-14th-2026) · [Value-model scoring](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/269340ce2eb8ca785bafa4f2c17a2e8b752bccd3/xai-value-model/scoring.rs)

Imagine a toy model with weights of +1 for a like and −10 for a report. For one viewer, a post's predictions of 20% and 1% contribute `0.20 − 0.10 = 0.10`. For another viewer, predictions of 4% and 2% contribute `0.04 − 0.20 = −0.16`. The post can have the same public engagement counts in both requests. The score changes because the predictions change. These example weights are invented to show the arithmetic.

The November 21 diff shows why the scoring mode matters too. In the multiplicative mode, the code moved two video-continuation terms into the engagement sum and left the time factor based on dwell time. Moving a term between factors can change how it interacts with other predictions even when its individual weight stays fixed. This is a change to a particular scoring branch in the source. The diff gives no traffic share for that branch. [November 21 scoring changes](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/commit/269340ce2eb8ca785bafa4f2c17a2e8b752bccd3) · [Current fusion functions](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/269340ce2eb8ca785bafa4f2c17a2e8b752bccd3/xai-value-model/scoring.rs)

## Reranking can account for neighboring posts

Home Mixer's `VMRanker` prepares local scores, including a cold-start adjustment where one applies. It can reuse a ranking response attached to the candidates or call the VM Ranker service. If the call fails, it can return the locally computed scores. If a response leaves out a candidate, that candidate retains its local score. The service can compute the value model and can apply a diversity step when the relevant configuration and runtime context enable it. [Home Mixer reranking scorer](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/269340ce2eb8ca785bafa4f2c17a2e8b752bccd3/home-mixer/scorers/vm_ranker.rs) · [Reranking service](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/269340ce2eb8ca785bafa4f2c17a2e8b752bccd3/vm-ranker/scoring/mod.rs)

One diversity method here is a determinantal point process, or DPP. It uses embeddings to compare posts and favors selections that combine a high score with less similarity. If the top two candidates cover the same match with nearly identical clips, a third candidate with a slightly lower score can move up because it adds something different. A configured history window can also bring recently served posts into that comparison. This is a separate decision from the transformer scoring each candidate against the viewer's history. [DPP implementation](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/269340ce2eb8ca785bafa4f2c17a2e8b752bccd3/vm-ranker/scoring/dpp_model.rs)

## Home Mixer builds the page after ranking the posts

The post pipeline selects candidates and applies post-selection filters. The outer `ForYouCandidatePipeline` takes the resulting posts as one source and collects other feed items, including ads, prompts, and Who to Follow modules. Its blender chooses an ad-placement strategy and inserts modules into the feed. A post's position on screen therefore reflects both the ordering among posts and the placement of the other items. [Post pipeline](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/269340ce2eb8ca785bafa4f2c17a2e8b752bccd3/home-mixer/candidate_pipeline/phoenix_candidate_pipeline.rs) · [Blending pipeline](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/269340ce2eb8ca785bafa4f2c17a2e8b752bccd3/home-mixer/candidate_pipeline/for_you_candidate_pipeline.rs) · [Blender selector](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/269340ce2eb8ca785bafa4f2c17a2e8b752bccd3/home-mixer/selectors/blender_selector.rs)

When reading a scoring change on GitHub, the repository's own example of the bidirectional-follow boost shows what to check. Follow the data that sets the feature, the parameter that enables or scales it, and the scorer that consumes it. The checked-in defaults provide a reference point. Feature switches and the applicable request path determine what actually runs for a viewer.
